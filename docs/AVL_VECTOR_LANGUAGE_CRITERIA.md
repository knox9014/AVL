# AVL vector language development criteria

The goal is an AI-learnable vector language. Human-readable syntax and symbolic reference semantics support measurement; they do not substitute for learned vector capability.

## Current layers
- Explicit name binding: copies instance identity without claiming new concept learning.
- Learned sender/receiver: finite four-relation meaning encoded in continuous vectors.
- Shared grounding: independently initialized same-family models share designed semantic coordinates.
- Reference semantics: AVL-L1 can check bounded spatial truth/negation, separately from learned vector inference.

## Completion criteria are empirical
1. Acquisition: measure new receiver sample/update costs including failures, not only final trained accuracy.
2. Composition: hold out semantic combinations, not merely names or surface wording; give every relation and relevant operator support in training.
3. Community transfer: independently trained models and eventually heterogeneous architectures, with explicit accounting for shared ontology, anchors, calibration and pretraining.
4. Cost: report weights/activation compute, training exposures, full wire metadata and symbolic/continuous baselines separately.
5. Robustness: distinguish nominal noise examples from worst-case robustness and unsupported-meaning detection.
6. Reproduction: preserve locked protocols, failed gates, data/source/checkpoint hashes and actual execution evidence.

## Next larger meaning space
Study a multi-factor continuous representation (e.g. directional offset plus magnitude) and independently decode its factors. Include a matched symbolic/factor baseline, equal-budget models, vector-norm controls and larger sampled learning curves. Separate taught numerical representation from novel rule inference. Withhold joint factor combinations under explicit symmetries so renamed duplicates cannot masquerade as new meanings.

Do not call a two-dimensional four-class codebook a globally optimal language. A larger vector alone is not greater meaning capacity unless its semantics are learned, tested and transferable. Any universal claim requires substantially broader tasks than the current spatial pilot.
